<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Aamu.app is an all-in-one productivity tool</p>
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Aamu.app is a connected workspace for teams: tasks, knowledge, communication, structured data, applications, and AI in one place.</p><p><strong>Company Brain in the age of AI.</strong></p><p>Do not spread your work across the Internet.</p><p>For years, choosing a separate app for every job seemed like the flexible way to build a company. Tasks went into a project tool, knowledge into a wiki, customer conversations into a helpdesk, meetings into a video service, structured data into spreadsheets, and code into yet another system. Each app could be good on its own. Together, they created a company whose memory was scattered across tabs, accounts, permissions, integrations, and search boxes.</p><p>That fragmentation is an even bigger limitation in the age of AI. An assistant can only understand the company context it can reach. If the work is split across disconnected services, the AI sees fragments too. It may produce fluent answers, but it does not share the same coherent picture as the team.</p><p>Aamu.app takes the opposite approach: <strong>one workspace and one mental model</strong> for the work, knowledge, communication, and applications of a company. When the context stays together, people can work with fewer interruptions and AI can become part of the real workflow instead of a separate chat window with incomplete information.</p><h2>One workspace, one mental model</h2><p>Projects, tasks, Docs, meetings, databases, forms, files, email, Helpdesk tickets, newsletters, design work, presentations, external applications, and code are not isolated categories in real life. They describe the same customers, products, decisions, deadlines, and responsibilities. Aamu keeps those relationships close instead of forcing the team to reconstruct them through integrations.</p><p>The interface follows the same principle. Once a team learns the main patterns for projects, navigation, editors, comments, permissions, and activity, that understanding carries across the workspace. Aamu is useful as a focused task and knowledge workspace on day one, and it can grow into the operational home for a whole team.</p><h2>Your whole company is on the same page</h2><p>A customer conversation can lead to a task. The task can point to the Doc that explains the decision. A meeting can record the discussion and action items. A database row can hold the structured customer or product data. The Activity feed can show what changed and link people back to the source.</p><p>This reduces a familiar kind of organizational drift: the document says one thing, the task board says another, and the most recent decision is trapped in a meeting or private inbox. In Aamu, the pieces can remain distinct without becoming disconnected.</p><h2>Tasks turn intention into accountable work</h2><p>Aamu Tasks supports lists, Kanban boards, calendar-oriented planning, reminders, comments, files, timers, custom fields, and statuses in the same workflow. Tasks live next to the information that gives them meaning: a support ticket can become follow-up work, a Doc can explain why the work matters, and a meeting can produce action items.</p><p>Tasks can also be created by forms, database automations, APIs, webhooks, or AI commands. That makes Tasks the operational center of the workspace rather than another isolated to-do list.</p><h2>Docs and databases give the work memory</h2><p>Aamu Docs are collaborative documents for plans, decisions, internal knowledge, specifications, meeting material, and publishing. Real-time editing, comments, snapshots, links, and sharing keep knowledge usable as it develops. Docs can be linked from tasks, tickets, database rows, and other workspace objects, and selected Docs can provide carefully maintained context for Company Brain.</p><p>Databases hold the structured side of the company. They can power a CRM, content workflow, internal tool, lightweight application, or public site. Tables support different ways to work with the same data, including grid and row views, linked records, activity history, and automations. New database dashboards can turn the data into a team-facing view with tables, cards, summaries, and charts.</p><p>Forms can write submissions directly into a database, while GraphQL, the REST API, API keys, and webhooks make the data available to external applications and automations. This blog demonstrates the model: its metadata lives in an Aamu Database, its articles are written in Aamu Docs, and an external build reads both through the API.</p><h2>Communication belongs beside the work</h2><p>Email, Helpdesk tickets, Live chat, video meetings, team chat, and newsletters are included because communication is not separate from operations. A customer question may reveal a bug, a missing document, a product request, or a process that needs to change.</p><p>Meetings can produce notes and tasks. Aamu's meeting AI can transcribe conversation with speaker information and update live notes containing summaries, decisions, topics, action items, and mentioned references. Support and email assistants can prepare drafts for review, while sending remains an explicit human-controlled action. Newsletters can be written and sent close to the source material and the team responsible for it.</p><h2>Slides, design, Git, and My Apps</h2><p>Aamu Slides brings spatial presentations into the project workspace. Decks are connected to projects and can be opened from Aamu, while deck creation can appear in the Activity feed. Team AI settings can also provide the Slides assistant with the team's configured provider without exposing provider credentials to the presentation application.</p><p>Penpot provides design and prototyping, and Git keeps repositories, issues, commits, pull requests, and code-related discussion close to product work. The newer <strong>My Apps</strong> area extends the workspace to team-owned applications: an external service can synchronize its items and activity into Aamu, use scoped API access, and open with Aamu authentication. The external app remains the source of its own data, while the team gets a connected entry point, permissions, and activity in the workspace.</p><h2>Company Brain: AI with the whole picture</h2><p><strong>Company Brain</strong> gives Aamu AI a permission-aware view across the workspace. A person can ask a question from Launch AI or mention <code>@ai</code> in the flow of work. Aamu can retrieve relevant context from tasks, Docs, meetings and transcripts, forms, file metadata, databases and rows, Helpdesk tickets, newsletters, projects, team members, Git work, and the signed-in user's own email.</p><p>That enables questions that do not fit inside a single app:</p><ul><li><p>What did we decide about onboarding, and is there already a task for it?</p></li><li><p>What have we promised this customer?</p></li><li><p>Which meeting introduced the new deadline?</p></li><li><p>Where is the current process documented?</p></li><li><p>Do we already have structured data for this company?</p></li></ul><p>Answers can link back to the original Aamu objects, so AI does not become a competing source of truth. It helps people find and understand the work; the task, Doc, meeting, ticket, or database row remains the source.</p><p>Permissions are part of retrieval. Company-level knowledge stays within the team, project information is limited to projects the person can access, and user-owned sources such as email have an additional owner check. The model is given only the context the signed-in person is allowed to see. Teams can configure their AI provider and model at team level, including supported hosted providers and OpenAI-compatible local endpoints.</p><h2>AI should work inside the workflow</h2><p>Aamu does not treat AI as a destination where people paste company information. AI belongs inside the workspace, where it can help write Docs, answer questions, turn comments into actions, summarize meetings, and draft customer replies with the surrounding context already present.</p><p>AI commands can turn natural-language instructions into concrete workspace operations across tasks, Docs, Forms, meetings, Helpdesk, Email, files, databases, rows, and automations. Human control remains visible: generated support or email replies appear as drafts for review, and reversible content work is separate from actions such as sending a message or changing important data.</p><h2>The applications are one connected system</h2><ul><li><p><strong>Tasks</strong> for planning, ownership, and execution.</p></li><li><p><strong>Docs</strong> for collaborative knowledge and writing.</p></li><li><p><strong>Databases, Forms, and dashboards</strong> for structured information, data collection, and visibility.</p></li><li><p><strong>Email, Helpdesk, and Live chat</strong> for customer communication.</p></li><li><p><strong>Video meetings and chat</strong> for discussion, transcription, notes, and decisions.</p></li><li><p><strong>Newsletters</strong> for publishing to an audience.</p></li><li><p><strong>Slides and Penpot</strong> for presentations, design, and prototyping.</p></li><li><p><strong>Git</strong> for keeping code near product work.</p></li><li><p><strong>My Apps, APIs, and webhooks</strong> for extending the workspace.</p></li><li><p><strong>Company Brain and AI</strong> for working across the shared context.</p></li></ul><p>Not every company needs every application on the first day. The advantage is that a new workflow can grow inside the same system without creating another island of information.</p><h2>Keep control of your data</h2><p>Bringing work together should not mean giving up control. Aamu supports incremental daily backups to your own S3-compatible storage, data import and export, scoped API keys, and webhooks. APIs make major workspace features available to integrations and AI-assisted automation, while permissions and explicit scopes keep access bounded.</p><h2>Where to start</h2><p>Start with Tasks or with one workflow that is currently fragmented: customer support, product planning, a knowledge base, a content process, a database-backed form, or a presentation workflow. Bring the work and its immediate context into Aamu. Then connect the Docs, conversations, meetings, data, and applications that explain it.</p><p>The larger idea becomes clear as the workspace grows: Aamu.app is not a bag of productivity features. It is a Company Brain built from the work your company is already doing—one workspace, one mental model, and one coherent picture for people and AI.</p><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>What is Introduction to Aamu.app?</h3><p>Aamu.app is an all-in-one productivity tool</p><h3>Who is Introduction to Aamu.app for?</h3><p>Introduction to Aamu.app is most useful for small and growing teams evaluating productivity software.</p><h3>What does Introduction to Aamu.app include?</h3><p>The article covers the main capabilities, workflow, and practical considerations related to introduction to aamu.app.</p><h2>Related articles</h2><ul><li><a href="/blog/posts/aamuapp-as-an-ai-workspace-alternative-to-notion-ai-slack-ai-jira-and-zendesk/">Aamu.app as an AI workspace alternative to Notion AI, Slack AI, Jira, and Zendesk</a></li><li><a href="/blog/posts/all-the-productivity-apps-you-could-think-of-100/">144 productivity apps in 2026</a></li><li><a href="/blog/posts/git-belongs-in-the-workspace-code-issues-comments-team-awareness-aamuapp/">Git belongs in the workspace: code, issues, comments, and team awareness in Aamu.app</a></li><li><a href="/blog/posts/penpot-design-tool-feature-overview/">Penpot Design Tool - feature overview</a></li><li><a href="/blog/posts/ai-commands-in-aamuapp-turning-comments-into-workspace-actions/">AI commands in Aamu.app: turning comments into workspace actions</a></li></ul>
